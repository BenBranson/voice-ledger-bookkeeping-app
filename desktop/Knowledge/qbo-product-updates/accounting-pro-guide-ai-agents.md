# Accountant's guide to using QuickBooks AI features (Firm of the Future)

Author: Amanda Thompson. No publication date is shown on the page; page metadata suggests first published around 2025-08-13 and updated around 2026-04-03. Content refers to events in September 2025.

## What it is
A guide for accounting professionals to the AI agents on the Intuit platform in QBO and QuickBooks Online Accountant (QBOA). Intuit calls it the biggest QuickBooks update in over 10 years.

## What it does
Accounting Agent:
- "Ready to post": sets aside transactions it considers ready so they can be posted in batches.
- Context gathering: builds to-do lists of missing information and sends clients "magic links" to supply receipts and answers.
- Anomaly detection: flags period-over-period changes in reports that may be errors.
- AI reconciliation: upload a PDF statement; it is compared against the bank feed and the recorded transactions to surface discrepancies.

Bank feed:
- Redesigned with inline editing.
- Categorization suggestions with explanations of why.
- Bank statements can be uploaded as PDF or image and the transactions extracted.

Also named but not detailed: Payroll, Payments, Finance, Customer and Project Management agents.

## Availability
- In QBO and QBOA; rollout began in 2025.
- The new interface could temporarily be switched per client (in the client's books) or for all clients (QBOA Settings > Choose Client Version). Clients opted out were to be moved back into the new experience by the end of September (2025).
- QBOA: the Accountant toolbox is renamed "My Menu" and sits at the top of the left navigation, customizable.
- QuickBooks Bill Pay Basic is included with every QBO subscription, with five free ACH payments a month; Premium and Elite are upgrades.
- Intuit Accountant Suite (firm management) was in beta; a bank feeds feedback pilot ran through September 2025.

## What it means for a bookkeeper
- Reconciliation can start from a statement upload instead of ticking by hand.
- Categorization: review suggestions and their explanations; batch-post the Ready to post group.
- Close/cleanup: anomaly flags point to variances worth investigating; magic links replace some client email chasing.

## Limits / caveats
- The guide stresses built-in review and approval: the agent suggests, the accountant decides.
- No accuracy figures are given.
- It does not explain turning agents on or off, user roles or permissions.
