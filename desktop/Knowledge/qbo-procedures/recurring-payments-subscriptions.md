# Recurring Payments overview (subscription payments)

## Purpose / when to use
Charges a customer automatically on a fixed schedule (subscriptions, regular monthly fees) instead of creating an invoice by hand each period. QuickBooks collects the payment, notifies the customer and produces the receipt.

## Steps in QBO
1. Sales > Recurring Payment > Create Recurring Payment.
2. Enter the customer and the products/services to charge.
3. Tick the "One-time" column for any line that should appear only on the first charge (e.g. a setup fee).
4. Gear icon > Recurring Payment Settings. Choose when to charge:
   - Immediately: first charge happens on the day the customer consents; the customer has 30 days to approve.
   - Future date: charges begin on the date you set.
5. Set the interval (Daily, Weekly, Monthly or Annually) and how many intervals between charges.
6. Set a start date and an end date, or "Never" for open-ended.
7. Review and Send, confirm, and send to the customer for consent.

## Prerequisites and plan limits
- Active QBO subscription plus an active merchant services (QuickBooks Payments) account.
- Customer can pay by credit card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or ACH. PayPal and Venmo are not supported.
- The feature is labelled limited availability.

## Pitfalls
- A recurring payment cannot be duplicated; each is built individually.
- If the customer does not approve before the consent request expires, you must resend it. QuickBooks sends automatic reminders 7, 3 and 1 day before expiry.
- If a charge fails, the invoice is left unpaid; if the customer does not update the payment method by the next charge date, the recurring payment is cancelled automatically.

## How to verify
Sales & Get Paid > Recurring Payments lists each one with next charge date, amount and status: Active, Pending, Canceled, Completed, Not Paid or Not Accepted.
