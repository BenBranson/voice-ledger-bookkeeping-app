# Banking and Accounting Level 1: Lesson 3 - Frequent Tasks (Money-In Section)

## Overview
Lesson 3 covers frequent banking tasks in QuickBooks Online. The money-in section addresses how to handle payment transactions from customers and deposits them into bank accounts.

## Key Topics Covered

### 1. Using the Undeposited Funds Account

**What it is:**
- A pending account area in QuickBooks that holds customer payments before they are deposited into the bank
- Allows time for accumulation of multiple payments before creating a single bank deposit
- Affects the sales and customers workflow

**Why it matters:**
- Proper segregation of payments in and out before bank reconciliation
- Ensures accurate matching of bank deposits with customer records
- Prevents over-stating income when multiple small payments are combined into one bank deposit

**Problem when NOT used:**
- When a client receives three $50 payments from a customer and deposits them as one $150 check at the bank, without using undeposited funds account:
  - The $150 deposit may not properly match with the three individual $50 payments
  - Risk of income being overstated and customer accounts continuing to reflect open invoices
  - Creates reconciliation and accounting accuracy issues

### 2. Matching Money-In Transactions

**Process:**
- Money-in bank transactions (deposits) appear on the Bank transactions screen
- These transactions are automatically assigned to the undeposited funds account
- Bookkeeper reviews and matches these transactions appropriately

**Key actions:**
- Review bank deposits to identify which customer payments they represent
- Use the Bank transactions screen (with tabs: Pending list, Partial, Excluded)
- Match transactions to properly categorize money received

### 3. Categorizing Money-In Transactions

**General rule:**
- Usually only categorize money-in transactions when money doesn't involve a customer (rare cases)
- Most money-in should flow through the undeposited funds account to maintain customer records

**Exceptions that need categorization:**
- IRS reimbursements
- Owner contributions or capital deposits
- Other non-customer payments

**Importance:**
- Different clients have different situations; must be careful when categorizing
- Ask about each client's specific payment scenarios to ensure accuracy

### 4. QuickBooks Payments (Optional Payment Method)

**What it is:**
- A service that allows clients to accept payments online
- Customers can pay via PayPal, Venmo, Apple Pay, credit card, or ACH bank transfer
- Payments can be processed in-person or over the phone

**Key advantage:**
- Eliminates the need for the undeposited funds workflow
- Payment accounting happens automatically when payment is made
- Everything is reconciled automatically

**Workflow:**
- Payment is recorded in QuickBooks automatically
- Deposit into bank account is automatically matched with bank transaction
- Transaction fees are recorded and properly categorized

**Benefit:**
- Reduces accounting work since payments and accounting happen simultaneously
- Streamlines the entire money-in process
- Less manual reconciliation needed

**Additional resource:**
- A dedicated QuickBooks Payments self-paced course is available in ProAdvisor Academy for deeper learning

## Common Errors/Best Practices

- **DO** use undeposited funds account for customer payments
- **DON'T** allocate multiple customer payments directly to income without going through undeposited funds
- **CONSIDER** QuickBooks Payments for streamlined payment processing
- **VERIFY** that client payments properly match bank deposits before closing books

## Practice Scenario

- Craig's Design and Landscaping Services (sample account) is used for hands-on practice
- Practice involves identifying correct matches for bank transactions in the Bank transactions screen
- Covers realistic scenarios of payment matching and deposit reconciliation

## Summary

The money-in section of Lesson 3 emphasizes:
1. The critical role of the undeposited funds account in maintaining accounting accuracy
2. Proper matching of bank deposits to customer payments
3. Careful categorization of non-customer money-in transactions
4. Optional streamlining through QuickBooks Payments for new-generation payment processing

This foundation is essential for accurate bank reconciliation and customer account management.
