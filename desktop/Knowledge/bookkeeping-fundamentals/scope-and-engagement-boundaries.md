# Engagement boundaries and scope creep

## The boundary
The engagement agreement lists what is included and, just as important, what is not: invoicing and collections (accounts receivable work), bill entry and paying vendors (accounts payable work), payroll processing, tax filings, and moving client money. The bookkeeper reconciles and reports; the client authorizes and makes payments. Keeping those roles separate is a basic internal control.

## Scope creep
Small favors outside the agreement ("can you just run payroll this week") add up to unpaid work and add liability for work the firm is not contracted or insured to do. Every out-of-scope request should get a price before the work starts.

## Answering a request
Say what the current agreement covers, say the request is outside it, and offer a priced option: a monthly upgrade or a one-time charge at the out-of-scope rate, starting after the client approves in writing. Voice Ledger's Scope Requests page has preset prices and drafts this reply.

## Onboarding order
1. Agreement signed and first payment received.
2. Accountant access to QuickBooks and read-only bank access.
3. Prior tax return and the latest trial balance.
4. Communication rules: one channel (email or portal), response times, no financial requests by text.
