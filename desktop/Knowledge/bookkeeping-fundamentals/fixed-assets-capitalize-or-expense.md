# Capitalize or expense a purchase

## The question
Equipment, vehicles, furniture and improvements that last more than a year are normally recorded as fixed assets and depreciated. Small items are expensed.

## De minimis safe harbor (IRS)
A business can elect each year to expense tangible property costing up to 2,500 dollars per invoice or per item (5,000 dollars for businesses with an audited or applicable financial statement), as long as it treats them the same way in its own books. The election is a statement attached to the timely filed tax return; it is not a change of accounting method. It does not cover inventory or land. The CPA makes the election; the bookkeeper should know whether the client uses it and expense small items consistently.

## Bookkeeping practice
- Record a fixed asset at full cost including delivery and setup.
- Repairs that keep an asset running are expenses; improvements that extend its life or add value are capitalized.
- Keep the purchase invoice and, for vehicles and equipment, the financing paperwork.
- When an asset is sold or scrapped, remove its cost and accumulated depreciation and record any gain or loss; ask the CPA for the treatment.
- An asset bought with a loan: debit the asset for the full price, credit the loan for the financed amount and the bank for any down payment.
