# Bank and credit card reconciliation

## Purpose
Prove that the books match the bank. Every statement line should exist once in the books, and every cleared transaction in the books should be on the statement. Reconcile every bank and credit card account every month, in date order; never skip a month.

## Steps
1. Start with the statement's beginning balance. It must equal the last reconciliation's ending balance. If it doesn't, a previously reconciled transaction was changed or deleted.
2. Enter the statement ending balance and ending date.
3. Tick off each deposit and payment that appears on the statement.
4. The difference must reach exactly 0.00. Do not finish with a difference; QuickBooks will post the gap to a Reconciliation Discrepancies expense account, which hides the real error.
5. Save the reconciliation report with the month's records.

## Finding a difference
- Difference equals one transaction amount: that item is missing from the books or entered twice.
- Difference is divisible by 9: likely a transposition (e.g. 54 entered as 45).
- Difference is twice an amount: a deposit entered as a payment, or the reverse.
- Check for transactions dated in the wrong month, items cleared in a prior reconciliation that were later edited, and bank fees or interest not yet entered.
- Outstanding checks older than a few months should be investigated: voided, never cashed, or duplicated.

## Credit cards
Reconcile to the card statement's closing balance. Payments to the card are transfers from the bank account, not expenses. Each charge is the expense.

## Undeposited Funds
Customer payments received into Undeposited Funds must be grouped into a bank deposit that matches the bank's deposit amount. Old items sitting in Undeposited Funds usually mean the deposit was recorded separately as income, which double-counts it.
