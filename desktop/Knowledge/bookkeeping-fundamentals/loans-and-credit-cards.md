# Loans, lines of credit and credit cards

## Setting up a loan
Record the full loan amount as a liability (Notes Payable or a named loan account) when the money is received or the asset is financed. Long-term loans (over a year) are Long Term Liabilities; the part due within twelve months can be shown as current.

## Each loan payment
Split it: principal reduces the loan liability, interest goes to Interest Expense. Use the lender's statement or amortization schedule. Posting the whole payment to expense overstates expenses; posting it all to the loan drives the loan balance negative over time.

## Checking the balance
Once a month or quarter, compare the loan account to the lender's statement and adjust interest if needed.

## Lines of credit
Draws increase the liability; payments reduce it, with interest split out the same way.

## Credit cards
- Set the card up as a Credit Card account (a liability).
- Each charge is an expense (or asset) on the card account.
- A payment to the card is a transfer from the bank to the card, never an expense.
- Refunds on the card reduce the original expense category.
- Reconcile to the monthly statement.
