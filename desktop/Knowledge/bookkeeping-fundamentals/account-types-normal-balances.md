# Account types and normal balances

## Balance sheet accounts (carry forward month to month)
- Bank and cash: normal debit balance. Negative means overdrawn, or deposits missing, or a payment posted to the wrong bank.
- Accounts Receivable: debit. Negative usually means a customer payment with no invoice, or a credit memo never applied.
- Other current assets (prepaid expenses, Undeposited Funds, inventory): debit.
- Fixed assets (vehicles, equipment, buildings): debit; Accumulated Depreciation is a contra account with a credit balance.
- Accounts Payable: credit. Negative means bills paid that were never entered, a payment applied twice, or a vendor credit not matched to a bill.
- Credit cards: credit. Negative means the payoff was recorded but some charges were not, or a payment was posted twice.
- Loans and notes payable: credit. Negative usually means the whole loan payment, interest included, was posted against principal, or the loan's opening balance was never entered.
- Payroll and sales tax liabilities: credit. Negative usually means tax payments were recorded as expense AND as liability payments, or the liability side was never booked.
- Equity (owner investment, retained earnings): credit. Owner draws/distributions carry a debit balance by design.

## Income statement accounts (reset each year into Retained Earnings)
- Income: credit. A debit balance usually means refunds or payments miscoded to income, or a deposit recorded twice and then reversed against income.
- Cost of goods sold and expenses: debit. A credit balance usually means a refund or vendor credit was coded to the expense with nothing to offset, or an expense was reversed twice.

## Reading the chart of accounts
Keep it short and specific. Watch for duplicates that differ only in spelling, accounts with no activity, and generic catch-alls (Uncategorized Expense, Ask My Accountant, Miscellaneous) that hold more than small, explained amounts at month end.
