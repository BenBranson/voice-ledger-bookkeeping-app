# Financial statements and ratios

## The three statements
- Profit and Loss (income statement): income minus expenses for a period, ending in net income.
- Balance Sheet: what the business owns (assets), owes (liabilities) and the owner's stake (equity) on a date. Assets always equal liabilities plus equity.
- Statement of Cash Flows: why cash changed, split into operating, investing and financing activity. Profit and cash differ because of receivables, payables, loan principal, asset purchases and owner draws.

## Ratios Voice Ledger shows
- Working capital = Total Current Assets minus Total Current Liabilities. Positive means short-term assets cover short-term debts.
- Current ratio = Total Current Assets divided by Total Current Liabilities. Around 1.5 to 2 is generally comfortable; under 1 is a warning.
- Quick ratio = (cash plus accounts receivable) divided by Total Current Liabilities. Leaves out inventory and prepaids; around 1 or higher is generally comfortable.
- Gross margin = Gross Profit divided by Total Income. 100 percent means no Cost of Goods Sold accounts are used, common for service businesses that book labor and materials as ordinary expenses.
- Net margin = Net Income divided by Total Income.

## Reading them with care
Ratios are only as good as the books. Uncategorized items, negative balances, and unreconciled accounts distort every ratio, so clean the books first and then interpret.
