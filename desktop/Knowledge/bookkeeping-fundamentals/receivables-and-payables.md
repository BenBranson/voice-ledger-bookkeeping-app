# Receivables and payables

## Accounts Receivable (what customers owe)
- The aging report buckets open invoices by days past due: current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90, over 90.
- Every customer payment should be applied to the invoice it pays. A payment recorded as a deposit straight to income leaves the invoice open and double-counts income.
- Credit memos and unapplied payments show as negative amounts on the aging; apply them to open invoices.
- Over 90 days: confirm with the client whether it is collectible. Uncollectible invoices are written off with a credit memo or journal entry to Bad Debt Expense, on accrual books only; a cash-basis business never recorded the income, so there is nothing to deduct.
- Customer statements help collections.

## Accounts Payable (what the business owes vendors)
- Enter the bill when it arrives, pay it later with Pay Bills so the payment is linked to the bill.
- A bill paid with a check written directly (not through Pay Bills) leaves the bill open and records the expense twice.
- Vendor credits reduce what is owed; apply them to the next bill.
- Old open bills: confirm with the client whether they were paid outside the books, are disputed, or are truly owed.
- Vendors paid as each charge happens (payroll services, many utilities, card charges) have no bills; zero owed is normal for them.

## Duplicate vendors and customers
Two records for the same vendor split the history and hide spending. Merge them in QuickBooks by renaming one to exactly match the other's name; the merge cannot be undone.
