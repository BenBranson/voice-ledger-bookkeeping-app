# Texas sales and use tax basics

## Rates
Texas charges a 6.25 percent state sales and use tax on retail sales, leases and rentals of most goods and on taxable services. Cities, counties, special purpose districts and transit authorities can add up to 2 percent, for a combined maximum of 8.25 percent. The local rate depends on the location of the sale.

## Filing frequency and due dates
The Comptroller assigns monthly, quarterly or yearly filing when the permit is approved.
- Monthly: due the 20th of the following month.
- Quarterly: April 20 (January-March), July 20 (April-June), October 20 (July-September), January 20 (October-December).
- Yearly: January 20 for the prior year.
A due date on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day.

## Bookkeeping
- Sales tax collected is a liability, not income. It should go to a Sales Tax Payable account (QuickBooks' sales tax feature does this).
- Paying the return reduces the liability; it is not an expense, except any penalty or interest.
- Compare taxable sales on the return to the books every period.
- Use tax is owed on taxable items bought without paying Texas tax (for example from an out-of-state seller); record it as an expense and a liability.
- Services: many services are taxable in Texas and many are not. Landscaping, for example, needs checking per service. Confirm with the Comptroller's guidance or the CPA.
