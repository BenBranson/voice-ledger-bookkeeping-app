# Adjusting entries

## Why they exist
Adjusting entries put income and expenses in the month they belong to on an accrual basis. They are usually journal entries at month end.

## Accrued expenses
An expense incurred but not yet billed or paid (wages earned through month end, utilities used). Debit the expense, credit an accrued liability. Reverse it next month when the real bill arrives.

## Accrued income
Work done but not yet invoiced. Debit a receivable, credit income. Most small businesses simply invoice promptly instead.

## Prepaid expenses
Paid now, used up over time (annual insurance, annual software). Record the payment to Prepaid Expenses (an asset), then each month move one month's share to the expense: debit Insurance Expense, credit Prepaid Expenses.

## Unearned revenue (customer deposits)
Cash received before the work is done. Credit a liability (Customer Deposits or Unearned Revenue); move it to income as the work is delivered.

## Depreciation
Fixed assets are expensed over their useful life. Monthly: debit Depreciation Expense, credit Accumulated Depreciation. Tax depreciation (often accelerated) is the CPA's area; the books may use the CPA's year-end figures.

## Loan payments
Each payment has a principal part and an interest part. Use the lender's statement or amortization schedule: debit the loan liability for principal, debit Interest Expense for interest, credit the bank for the total.
