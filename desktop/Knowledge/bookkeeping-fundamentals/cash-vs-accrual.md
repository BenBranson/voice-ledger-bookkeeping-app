# Cash basis vs accrual basis

## Definitions (IRS Publication 583)
- Cash method: report income when it is received and deduct expenses when they are paid.
- Accrual method: report income when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, whether or not money has changed hands.
A business must use a method that clearly shows its income and use it consistently. Changing methods for tax generally needs IRS approval. Businesses with inventory generally must account for purchases and sales of inventory on the accrual method, with exceptions for qualifying small businesses.

## What changes on the reports
- On accrual, open invoices count as income and open bills count as expenses for the month they are dated.
- On cash, the same invoice counts only when the payment lands; Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable do not appear on a cash-basis balance sheet.
- QuickBooks Online keeps both views; each report has a Cash/Accrual setting. The company default is set in Account and Settings, Advanced, Accounting.

## Bookkeeping implications
- Comparing a client's tax return to QuickBooks reports: make sure the report basis matches the return's method.
- Entering bills and invoices (rather than only recording payments) is what makes accrual reports meaningful.
- A cash-basis client who never enters bills will show zero Accounts Payable even when they owe vendors money.
