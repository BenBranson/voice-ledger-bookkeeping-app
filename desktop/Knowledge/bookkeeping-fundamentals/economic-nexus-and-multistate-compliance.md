# Economic nexus and multi-state compliance

## What it is
Since the 2018 Supreme Court decision in South Dakota v. Wayfair, a state can require an out-of-state seller to collect its sales tax once the seller's sales into that state pass a threshold, even with no office there. That is economic nexus.

## Thresholds
Most states use 100,000 dollars of sales into the state over a year; some also count transactions. Checked examples: California 500,000 dollars of sales delivered into the state in the current or preceding calendar year; Texas 500,000 dollars of Texas revenue over the preceding twelve months; New York more than 500,000 dollars AND more than 100 sales over the last four sales tax quarters. Always check the specific state's rule.

## Marketplaces
Amazon, Etsy, eBay and similar marketplaces collect and remit sales tax for their sellers in nearly every state. Sales through the seller's own website or invoices are the seller's responsibility.

## Services
Many states tax few services; nexus questions mostly arise for sellers of goods and taxable services.

## The bookkeeper's role
Track sales by customer state (Voice Ledger's Compliance Calendar shows the last 12 months), flag states approaching or over a threshold, and bring them to the client and CPA. Registering in a new state is the client's decision with the CPA's advice.

## Other multi-state items
Employees working in another state usually bring payroll registration there; an office or warehouse in another state creates nexus regardless of sales; and many states require an out-of-state LLC or corporation doing business there to register as a foreign entity.
