# Texas sales tax on a bookkeeper's own services

## Not taxable
Professional services where the bookkeeper applies accounting knowledge: preparing income statements, balance sheets and profit and loss statements, reconciliations, and preparing tax returns. Using a computer to do the work does not make it taxable.

## Taxable: data processing
Entering, storing, manipulating or retrieving a customer's data is a taxable data processing service. 20 percent of the charge is exempt, so tax applies to 80 percent of it at the state rate of 6.25 percent plus any local rate. Plain data entry, such as typing a stack of bills or invoices into the system for the client, can fall here.

## Mixed charges: the 5 percent rule
If more than 5 percent of a single fee is for taxable services, the whole charge is taxable unless the taxable and nontaxable parts are stated separately on the invoice.

## What this means for the firm
- Keep the engagement focused on reconciliation, review, reporting and advice.
- If an add-on is mostly data entry (bill entry, invoice entry, payroll data input), list it as a separate line on the invoice and ask the CPA whether to collect sales tax on it.
- This note is general information; confirm the firm's own situation with a CPA or the Comptroller.
