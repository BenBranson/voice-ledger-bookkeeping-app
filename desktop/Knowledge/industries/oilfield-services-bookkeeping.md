# Oilfield services bookkeeping

## Job costing
Revenue and costs need to be tracked by customer (operator), lease or well, and job. Use QuickBooks customers/projects for operator and job, and classes or locations for service line or yard. Invoice by job ticket and keep the ticket number on the invoice.

## Equipment
- Heavy equipment and trucks are fixed assets; keep a schedule with cost, in-service date and depreciation.
- Equipment rented from others: Equipment Rental expense, tracked by job when billed through.
- Equipment rented to customers: separate rental income account; track hours or days per job.
- Repairs and maintenance by unit help decide when to replace a truck.

## Revenue issues
- Operators often pay 60 to 90 days out; watch receivables aging by operator.
- Retainage or holdbacks are a receivable until released.
- Fuel surcharges and mileage billed through: separate income lines.

## Costs
Labor by job, per diem, fuel, chemicals and materials, subcontractors (1099s), insurance, permits, safety training, and disposal fees. Overhead shared across jobs (yard, shop, a vacuum truck used on several wells) can be allocated by hours or miles if the client wants true job margins.

## Texas items
- Franchise tax: most oilfield service entities are LLCs or corporations; the Public Information Report is due May 15 even under the no tax due threshold.
- Sales tax: some oilfield services and rentals are taxable in Texas and some are not; confirm by service with the CPA or the Comptroller.
- Workers: classify crews correctly as employees or contractors.

## Red flags
One big income account with no job detail, equipment purchases expensed, fuel cards not reconciled, and invoices that don't match field tickets.
