# Hot shot trucking bookkeeping

## The core problem
A settlement sheet shows the gross rate for a load, but the owner-operator keeps far less after fuel, factoring fees, tolls, scale tickets and maintenance. Gross deposits are not profit.

## Revenue
- Record the full gross freight revenue per load, not the net amount the factoring company deposits.
- Factoring fees are an expense (Factoring Fees), not a reduction hidden inside revenue. Reserve amounts held back by the factor are an asset (Factoring Reserve Receivable) until released.
- Detention pay, layover and accessorial charges: track them separately; compare the broker's rate confirmation to the settlement to catch missing detention.
- Fuel surcharge income can be its own income account.

## Expenses to keep separate
Fuel, tolls and scale tickets, truck and trailer repairs and maintenance, tires, insurance (liability, cargo, physical damage), permits and licenses, ELD and dispatch fees, load board subscriptions, lumper fees, truck payments (principal to the loan, interest to Interest Expense), per diem or meals per the CPA.

## Profitability by load or lane
Use QuickBooks classes or tags (or customer/project) for each load or lane so revenue and direct costs can be compared per run.

## Compliance dates
- IFTA quarterly fuel tax returns: April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31. Keep miles by state and fuel receipts.
- IRS Form 2290 heavy vehicle use tax: due August 31 for vehicles of 55,000 pounds or more gross weight (tax period July 1 to June 30). Many hot shot setups are lighter; check the registered gross weight.

## Red flags
Deposits from a factoring company booked straight to income at the net amount, fuel on personal cards, no maintenance reserve, and truck loan payments posted entirely to expense.
