# Grocery and convenience stores

## Why they are hard
Hundreds of daily sales, many vendor deliveries (some paid in cash at the door), taxable and non-taxable items mixed together, lottery and money orders, EBT, and large inventory. Price these at the top tier or decline unless the store has a working point-of-sale system.

## Daily sales journal
Never record register tickets one by one. Book one daily journal from the point-of-sale summary: taxable sales, non-taxable sales, sales tax, lottery and money order collections, EBT, card and cash, and cash over/short. Match each bank and card settlement to it.

## Other people's money
Lottery, money orders and bill-payment collections belong to others; run them through clearing or liability accounts, never income.

## Inventory
Rely on the point-of-sale system's item counts. Compare cost of goods to sales every month; a sudden change means missing purchases, missing sales or shrinkage. Adjust the books to a physical count at least once a year.

## Vendor-direct deliveries
Bread, beer, chips and soda vendors often deliver to the shelf and are paid by check or cash on delivery. Make sure those purchases are entered.

## Red flags
Growing cash over/short, lottery booked as sales, cost of goods jumping without price changes, and cash-paid deliveries never entered.
