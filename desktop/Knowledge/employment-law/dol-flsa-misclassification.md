# Misclassification of Employees as Independent Contractors Under the FLSA (US Department of Labor)

## What it is
Misclassification means treating a worker who is legally an employee as an independent contractor. It denies the worker protections they are owed.

## Consequences (as stated)
A misclassified worker can miss out on minimum wage, overtime pay and other legal protections and benefits.

## Rule referenced on the page
- Final rule: "Employee or Independent Contractor Classification Under the Fair Labor Standards Act", published January 10, 2024, effective March 11, 2024, codified at 29 CFR Part 795.
- It rescinded the earlier 2021 Independent Contractor rule (published January 7, 2021, 86 FR 1168).
- The page itself does not list the classification factors; it points to the rule and resources below.
- The page (as retrieved 2026-09-27) does not mention any later change in enforcement policy.

## Bookkeeper note (not from source)
- The 2024 rule uses an "economic reality" test weighing six factors: opportunity for profit or loss from managerial skill; investments by the worker and the employer; permanence of the relationship; nature and degree of control; whether the work is integral to the employer's business; and skill and initiative. No single factor decides it.
- In May 2025 DOL announced (Field Assistance Bulletin 2025-1) that it would stop applying the 2024 rule in its own investigations and use older guidance instead, and said it planned to rescind the rule. Check the DOL site for the current status. The FLSA test is separate from the IRS common-law test used for payroll tax and 1099 purposes. Classification is a legal question; refer clients to an attorney or CPA.
