# Get answers to your 1099 questions

Article last updated 8/5/2026. No specific tax year is named; it speaks of payments made "last year".

## Purpose / when to use
Quick answers on whether a business must file 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC, when, and how.

## Key points (as stated)
Who gets one:
- 1099-NEC: non-employees (contractors, freelancers, some vendors) paid $600 or more in the prior year, and anyone subject to backup withholding.
- 1099-MISC: miscellaneous payments of $600 or more in the prior year.

Deadlines:
- 1099-NEC: January 31 (to the IRS and to the contractor).
- 1099-MISC: March 31 if filed electronically; contractors should receive their copy by January 31.
- If a due date lands on a weekend, it moves to the next business day.

E-filing:
- If you have 10 or more information returns in total (1099s, W-2s and other federal forms combined), you must file electronically.
- You can e-file directly with the IRS through IRIS, or through QuickBooks for a fee.

Exclusions:
QuickBooks automatically leaves out payments that do not belong on a 1099, such as those made by credit card and other electronic payment methods (see payments-excluded-1099.md).

## Bookkeeper note (not from source)
- The $600 thresholds apply to payments made through tax year 2025. For payments made after December 31, 2025 (tax year 2026 forms filed in early 2027), the One Big Beautiful Bill Act raises the 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC threshold to $2,000, indexed for inflation afterward. The article had not been updated for this as of its 8/5/2026 revision. Check irs.gov for the year you are filing.
- Paper-filed 1099-MISC is due to the IRS by February 28; the March 31 date is for e-filing.
